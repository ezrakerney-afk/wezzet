How Morse code works

Morse code spells each letter as short signals (dots) and long signals (dashes). It was developed in the 1830s and 1840s for the electric telegraph, and the most common letters got the shortest codes: E is one dot and T is one dash.

Timing

A dash lasts three dots. The gap between signals in a letter is one dot, between letters three dots and between words seven dots.

Well-known codes

Morse is still used by amateur radio operators, in aviation navigation beacon identifiers and as an accessibility input method.
