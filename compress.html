    Questions

    How does it hit the size?

    It tries quality levels until the file fits under your target. If even low quality is too big, it makes the image a little smaller and tries again.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Compression happens in your browser.

    Why JPG or WebP and not PNG?

    PNG has no quality setting, so it can't be squeezed to an exact size. WebP keeps transparency and is usually smaller than JPG.

    About image compression

    Compression makes an image file smaller. Lossy compression (JPG, and WebP by default) removes detail the eye barely notices; lossless compression (PNG) keeps every pixel.

    Choosing a quality

    For JPG, a quality of 75 to 85 usually looks the same as the original at a fraction of the size. Below about 60, blocky artifacts start to show around edges and text.

    Dimensions matter most

    Halving the width and height cuts the pixel count to a quarter. Resize to the size you will display before compressing.

    Why bother

    Smaller images load faster, use less mobile data and fit under email and upload limits. Page speed also affects search ranking.

    Repeated lossy saves add up, so keep your original and compress a copy.
