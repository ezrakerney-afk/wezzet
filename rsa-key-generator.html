About RSA keys

RSA, published by Rivest, Shamir and Adleman in 1977, is a public-key system. It uses a pair of keys: a public key you can share with anyone and a private key you keep secret. Data encrypted with one can only be decrypted with the other, and its security rests on the difficulty of factoring the product of two large primes.

Key sizes

2048 bits is the minimum recommended today; 3072 or 4096 bits give a longer safety margin at the cost of slower operations.

Formats

Keys are usually saved as PEM text, which begins with a line such as BEGIN PUBLIC KEY.

Handling

Never share or paste a private key anywhere you do not control. If it leaks, generate a new pair and replace the public key everywhere it was installed.
